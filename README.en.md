# Where does the data come from?

A presentation on the codes and classifications that macroeconomic statistics rest on: where
the international standards come from, how they reach Russian practice, who is responsible
for the transposition, and what changes in the next few years.

## What is in it

Seven sections.

**Introduction** — why classifications are needed, and the three questions worth asking of any
code: what is being classified, which version, and who carries out the transposition.

**Families of classifications** — the three storeys of the system: international standard,
regional version, national classification. Who writes the standards, and how many digits
the codes share.

**Economic classifications** — the SNA, the balance of payments, GFS and COFOG, ISIC, CPC, HS,
COICOP. For each: what it is, how its versions evolved, and what the next one changes. Separate
slides cover where international organisations get their data and what they do with it before
publishing.

**People, units, territories** — occupations, education, sectors, forms of ownership,
territorial division and units of measurement.

**Russia** — what is inherited, what is domestic, and what is changing. OKVED 3 and OKPD 3 from
2029, the reform of how codes are assigned, the registers, the budget classification, the
consumer basket, and a tax bill with its imprint on the statistics.

**Statistics and company accounts** — why profit under IFRS and the profit of an economy do not
agree, and where formal bridging tables exist and where they do not.

**Where this breaks** — breaks in series when versions change, the limits of correspondence
tables, double coding, and what even the newest standards leave unresolved.

An appendix at the end holds a glossary of abbreviations, international and Russian.

One object runs through the whole deck: an oil refinery that collects two dozen codes along the
way. The second-to-last slide shows its full passport.
